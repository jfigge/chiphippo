The Gallery

The gallery is a collection of circuits built in Chip Hippo, at chiphippo.com/gallery. You can open any of them on your own desk to run it, take it apart, or build on it, and you can share a design of your own for others to find.

Browsing the gallery

Choose File ▸ Browse Projects… (Shift+Cmd/Ctrl+B), or click the grid button at the end of the toolbar's file buttons, and pick the window's Gallery tab (its other tab, Recent Projects, shows your own recent projects — see Projects & Desktops). The gallery shows one card for each design, showing its picture, who made it, its level (Beginner, Intermediate or Advanced), which engine it is built for, and how many chips and parts it has.

Narrow the list with the search box (it matches titles, names and tags) and the three filters beside it: a tag, a level, and the engine (Logic or Spice Lite). Choose Open on a card to put that design on your desk.

The first time, the Gallery tab asks you to Allow… Chip Hippo to read the gallery. Browsing reads the list of designs and their pictures from chiphippo.com, and a design when you open one; nothing of yours is sent. Choose Allow to go on, or Not now to leave it. You can change the answer later in Settings ▸ Gallery ▸ Gallery access.

Opening a design

A design from the gallery arrives as a new desktop in the project you have open, named after the design, so nothing already on your desk is touched. Open the same design again and Chip Hippo switches to the desktop it is already on rather than adding a second copy. It is yours from then on: change it, save it with the project, or export it.

A design that uses custom chips brings them along. They join the open project only, not your chip library, so browsing never changes the parts tray for your other projects. If the project already has an older revision of one of those chips, Chip Hippo asks what to do: Update uses the new revision everywhere the project places that chip, and Keep Both adds it as a second chip.

Before a design lands, Chip Hippo checks that it will open as it was built:

  • A design published for an older version of Chip Hippo is checked against this one. If anything would be lost — a part, a wire, a custom chip that can no longer run — the message names it, and you can Open Anyway to see what remains, or leave it.
  • A design built for Spice Lite while the logic engine is on offers to Turn On Spice Lite (Settings ▸ Simulator), so it runs as designed, or to Open Without it.
  • A design saved by a newer version of Chip Hippo than yours can't be opened; update the app first.

From the website

Each design on chiphippo.com/gallery has three buttons:

  • Open in browser opens it in Chip Hippo in the browser, with nothing to install.
  • Open in desktop app hands it to Chip Hippo on your computer, if you have it installed. It opens as a new desktop, with the same checks as above.
  • Download saves its .desktop.chiphippo file. Double-click the file to open it in Chip Hippo, or bring it into a project with Desktop ▸ Import Desktop….

Sharing a design

Any desktop can be shared to the gallery, from the desktop app or from Chip Hippo in the browser. Choose Desktop ▸ Share to Gallery…, or right-click a desktop's tab and choose Share to Gallery… to share that one. Stop the circuit first; sharing is greyed out while it runs.

The card asks for four things:

  • Title — what the design is called in the gallery (up to 80 characters).
  • Your name — the name shown with it (up to 40 characters). Use whatever name you are happy to see published; it need not be your real one. Chip Hippo remembers it for next time.
  • Description — what it does and how to use it (optional, up to 1,000 characters).
  • Tags — up to eight words, separated by commas, that help people find it, such as counter, 555. A tag is letters, digits and hyphens.

Below the fields the card says whether the design is ready: it is checked the way the gallery will check it, so an empty desktop, or one that would not open whole from the gallery, can't be sent, and the card says why.

Then it says what sharing means, and Share Publicly sends it:

  • What is sent: this desktop's design — its boards, parts, wiring, notes, the contents of any ROM on it, and the custom chips it uses — the four fields, and the version of Chip Hippo. Nothing else from your computer.
  • It is reviewed before it appears, and may be turned down.
  • Once approved it is public: anyone can open and download it, under the name you gave.

When it has gone, Chip Hippo says Sent for review. Your design appears in the gallery once it is approved.

Seeing what became of a share

Every design you share is listed in Settings ▸ Gallery under Your shares, newest first, with the date you shared it. Press Check status to find out where each one stands:

  • Waiting for review — it hasn't been looked at yet.
  • Accepted — it has been approved, and appears in the gallery once the website next updates.
  • Not accepted — it was turned down.

If the reviewer left a note — what to change, or why it wasn't taken — it is shown under the share as Reviewer's note.

Checking asks the gallery's service about your own shares and nothing else, and only when you press the button. The list is kept on this computer (in the browser version, in this browser), so shares made somewhere else aren't in it.

Shares stay on the list until you take them off: point at one and press its ×. That only tidies the list — it doesn't withdraw the share, which stays in review or in the gallery. (The list keeps your 50 most recent shares.)

Each share is a new design in the gallery. To publish a changed version, share it again under a new title.

If a share is refused

  • This design is already waiting for review — the same design under the same title is in the queue already; there is nothing more to do.
  • Too many shares from this connection — one connection can share five designs an hour. Try again later.
  • The gallery has too many designs waiting for review — the review queue is full. Try again later.
  • The gallery refused some of the wording — change the title, name, description or tags.
  • This design is larger than the gallery allows — a shared design can be up to 512 KB; large ROM images are the usual cause.
  • The gallery couldn't be reached — check the connection and try again.

Settings ▸ Gallery

  • Gallery access — whether Chip Hippo may read the gallery (Yes or No). It is asked the first time you browse, so you seldom need to set it here. Chip Hippo in the browser is already on the gallery's website, so it has no such setting.
  • Your shares — the designs you've shared, and Check status (above).
  • Share consent — what Share to Gallery shows before it sends:
    • Ask (the default) shows the full notice above every time.
    • Yes shows it in one line.
    • No switches sharing off; Share to Gallery then points you here.

Privacy

Browsing sends nothing of yours. Sharing sends only what the card lists, to the gallery's own service, which sees your IP address as any website does and keeps only a scrambled form of it, for an hour, to limit how often one address can share. Checking a share's status sends only that share's own reference. The privacy statement has the details, including how to have a shared design taken down.
